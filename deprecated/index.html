Rochester: A City Built on Innovation

Rochester was a tech city before tech cities existed. Scroll through two centuries of relentless invention.

1825

The Erie Canal

Built by muscle alone, the canal linked the Great Lakes to New York Harbor and put Rochester at the crossroads of American commerce. A village became a boomtown overnight.

1853

Bausch & Lomb

Two immigrants perfected mass-produced optical lenses — for spectacles, then microscopes, then cameras. Rochester became the world capital of precision optics.

1888

Eastman Kodak

"You press the button, we do the rest." George Eastman democratized photography. 60,000+ Rochester employees. 90% US film market share. A verb, a noun, a way of life.

1935

Kodachrome

Color film arrives. Rochester gives the world color photography — and the ability to preserve not just moments, but the warmth and life within them.

1959

Xerox 914

The first plain-paper copier ships from Rochester — labeled "No. 1, Ship to Boston." Xerox becomes a verb. Its research center later invents the GUI, the mouse, and Ethernet.

Photo: Democrat and Chronicle

2026

The Next Chapter

Same audacity, new model. World-class universities, deep talent, and hundreds of startups writing Rochester's next act. This time, the innovation is distributed.
