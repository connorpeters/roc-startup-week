200 Years of Audacity

Rochester was a tech city before tech cities existed.

From the Erie Canal to Kodak to Xerox — this city has always been built by people who saw what others couldn't.

1825

The Erie Canal

Built entirely by muscle power, the Erie Canal linked the Great Lakes to New York Harbor — and put Rochester at the crossroads of American commerce. The city exploded from a village to a boomtown, earning the nickname "The Flour City" as Midwestern grain flowed through its mills.

1888

Eastman Kodak

George Eastman transformed photography from a professional practice into a universal human experience. "You press the button, we do the rest." At its peak, Kodak employed over 60,000 in Rochester and held 90% of the US film market. The company's name became synonymous with capturing life itself.

1959

Xerox Ships the 914

The first automatic plain-paper copier rolled off the line in Rochester, labeled "No. 1 — Ship to Boston." Xerox didn't just create a product — it created a verb. The company's later research center invented the graphical user interface, the mouse, and Ethernet.

Photo: Democrat and Chronicle

2026

The Next Chapter

Rochester's story isn't a eulogy — it's a setup. World-class universities, deep technical talent, and the same audacity that built a canal through wilderness. The difference: this time, innovation is distributed across hundreds of startups rather than concentrated in three giants.
